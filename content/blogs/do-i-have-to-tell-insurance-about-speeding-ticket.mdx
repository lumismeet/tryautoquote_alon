In most cases, no, you don't have to call your insurer the day you get a speeding ticket. But that doesn't mean the ticket stays hidden. Insurers check your driving record when your policy renews, and they will see it then. What matters is answering honestly whenever they ask, and knowing the few situations where you do have to tell them sooner.

## Why Your Insurer Will Find Out Anyway

When you pay a ticket or are found guilty, it usually goes on your driving record, which your state's motor vehicle department keeps. Insurers pull that record, often called your motor vehicle report, when you apply for a policy and typically again at renewal. So even if you say nothing, a ticket on your record will normally show up within a policy term or two.

## When You Do Have to Tell Your Insurer

- **When they ask.** Applications and many renewal forms ask about recent tickets and violations. You must answer truthfully. Leaving a ticket out is misrepresentation, which can lead an insurer to cancel your policy or deny a claim.
- **If your license is suspended or revoked.** Most policies require you to report a change in license status, and a suspension can affect whether your coverage stays in force.
- **If your policy says so.** Some policies require you to report changes that affect your risk. Check the conditions section of yours.

## How a Speeding Ticket Affects Your Rate

A ticket usually raises your premium at your next renewal, not immediately. How much depends on:

- **How far over the limit you were.** A few miles over is treated very differently from excessive speed or a reckless driving charge.
- **Your record before the ticket.** A first minor ticket on a clean record usually costs less than a second or third.
- **Your insurer and your state.** Every company prices violations its own way.

Most insurers look back three to five years, so a ticket can affect your rate for several renewals before it drops off.

## Ways to Limit the Damage

- **Ask about traffic school.** In some states, completing a defensive driving course can keep a minor ticket off your record or reduce its points.
- **Consider contesting it.** If you believe the ticket was wrong, a dismissed ticket never reaches your record.
- **Check for violation forgiveness.** Some insurers waive the surcharge for a first minor violation, either automatically or as an add-on.
- **Shop around at renewal.** If your rate goes up, compare other insurers. They weigh tickets differently, so another company may charge noticeably less. See [how to compare car insurance quotes](/blogs/how-to-compare-auto-insurance).

## Points on Your License vs. Your Insurance Rate

Many states use a points system to track violations, and too many points can lead to a suspended license. Insurers generally don't use your state's points directly. They look at the violations on your record and apply their own pricing, so a ticket can raise your rate even if your state assigns it few points.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will one speeding ticket raise my insurance?

Often, yes, usually starting at your next renewal. A minor first ticket on an otherwise clean record typically has a smaller effect than serious or repeat violations, and some insurers forgive a first minor ticket.

### How long does a speeding ticket affect car insurance?

Usually three to five years, depending on your insurer and state. After that, it generally stops affecting your premium.

### Can I hide a speeding ticket from my insurance company?

Not reliably, and you shouldn't try. Insurers check your driving record at renewal. If they ask and you leave a ticket out, that's misrepresentation, which can put your coverage at risk.

## Key takeaways

- You usually don't have to call your insurer right after a speeding ticket, but they will typically see it on your driving record at renewal.
- You must answer honestly whenever an insurer asks about violations, and report a suspended or revoked license.
- A ticket generally raises your rate at the next renewal, often for three to five years.
- Traffic school, contesting the ticket, violation forgiveness and shopping around can all limit the cost.
