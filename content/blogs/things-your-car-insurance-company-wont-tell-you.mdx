Your insurer isn't exactly hiding these. Most of them are in your policy or your state's rules. But they're rarely mentioned when you buy a policy or file a claim, and knowing them can save you money, get you a bigger payout, or keep you out of trouble. Here are ten worth knowing.

## 1. You Can Switch Anytime, and Usually Get Money Back

You're not locked in until renewal. You can cancel mid-policy and generally get back the unused part of your premium. Just ask about any cancellation fee, and make sure the new policy starts before the old one ends.

→ [Can you switch car insurance mid-policy?](/blogs/can-you-switch-car-insurance-mid-policy)

## 2. A Total-Loss Offer Isn't Final

When your car is totaled, the insurer offers its actual cash value. If the number looks low, you can push back with listings for comparable cars, records of recent repairs or upgrades, and an independent appraisal.

→ [Actual cash value vs. replacement cost](/blogs/actual-cash-value-vs-replacement-cost)

## 3. You May Be Owed for Your Car's Lost Value

A car that's been in an accident is often worth less even after a perfect repair. If someone else caused the crash, you may be able to claim that lost value, called diminished value, from their insurer.

→ [Diminished value claims](/blogs/diminished-value-claims)

## 4. Your Rate Can Go Up Even if You Never File a Claim

Rising repair and medical costs, approved rate increases in your state, more claims in your area, or an expired discount can all raise your premium at renewal, even with a spotless record.

→ [Why your car insurance went up](/blogs/why-your-car-insurance-went-up)

## 5. Some "Save Money" Driving Apps Can Raise Your Rate

Usage-based programs reward safe driving, but not all of them are discount-only. In some programs, hard braking, late-night driving or phone use can push your price up. Ask which kind it is before you sign up.

→ [Is usage-based insurance worth it?](/blogs/telematics-usage-based-insurance)

## 6. You Usually Don't Have to Give the Other Driver's Insurer a Recorded Statement

You have a duty to cooperate with your own insurer, but you're generally not obligated to give a detailed or recorded statement to the other driver's insurance company.

→ [What not to tell your insurance company](/blogs/what-not-to-tell-your-insurance-company)

## 7. A Denied Claim Can Be Appealed

A denial isn't the end. You can ask for the reason in writing, submit more evidence, and if that fails, file a complaint with your state's insurance department.

→ [How to appeal a denied claim](/blogs/how-to-appeal-a-denied-auto-insurance-claim)

## 8. Full Coverage on an Older Car Can Cost More Than It Could Pay Out

Collision and comprehensive only pay up to your car's current value, minus your deductible. On an older car, a year of premiums can approach what the coverage could ever return.

→ [When to drop full coverage (with calculator)](/blogs/when-to-drop-full-coverage)

## 9. Your Credit Can Affect Your Price as Much as Your Driving Does

In most states, insurers can use a credit-based insurance score, and it can move your premium more than almost any factor besides your driving record. A few states ban or limit the practice.

→ [Does your credit score affect car insurance rates?](/blogs/credit-score-insurance)

## 10. Lending Your Car Means Lending Your Insurance

In most cases, car insurance follows the car, not the driver. If a friend crashes your car, your policy usually pays first, and the claim can affect your rate.

→ [Who's covered when someone else drives your car](/blogs/who-is-covered-when-someone-else-drives-your-car)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are insurance companies required to tell you these things?

Many of these rules are written in your policy or set by state law, but insurers aren't required to point them out. Reading your policy and asking direct questions is the best protection.

### What's the easiest way to pay less for car insurance?

Comparing quotes from several insurers, because prices for the same driver can vary widely. Reviewing your coverage and deductible each year helps too.

## Key takeaways

- You can switch insurers anytime and usually get the unused premium back.
- Total-loss offers and claim denials can be challenged.
- Rates can rise without a claim, and some driving apps can raise them.
- Full coverage on an older car may not be worth its cost.
- Credit, your ZIP code and who drives your car all affect what you pay and what's covered.
