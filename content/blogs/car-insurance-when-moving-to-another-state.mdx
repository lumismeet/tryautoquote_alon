When you move to a new state, your car insurance has to move with you, but it doesn't happen automatically. Your policy is written for the state where your car is kept, so a move usually means updating your policy, or replacing it, and meeting your new state's rules for coverage, registration and your driver's license. Handle it in the right order and you avoid both a coverage gap and a claim problem.

## Why Your Address Matters to Your Insurer

Insurers price and write policies based on where your car is garaged, meaning where it is kept most of the time. Each state sets its own minimum coverage, its own rules on fault, and its own rate filings. If you move and keep a policy tied to your old address, your coverage may not match your new state's requirements, and an out-of-date address can give an insurer grounds to question a claim.

## Tell Your Insurer Before You Move

Call your insurer as soon as you know your new address and moving date. One of two things will happen:

- **If your insurer operates in your new state,** it will typically rewrite or update your policy for that state, usually with a new rate.
- **If it doesn't,** you'll need a new insurer there. Keep your current policy active until the new one starts, so you're never uninsured.

Your current policy generally still covers you while you drive to your new home, since U.S. auto policies cover you in other states.

## Your New State May Require Different Coverage

Minimum requirements vary a lot. Your new state may require higher liability limits, personal injury protection if it's a no-fault state, or uninsured motorist coverage. Coverage that was enough in your old state may not be legal in the new one. Check your new state's requirements on our [car insurance by state](/car-insurance#states) pages before you finalize a policy.

## Registration and Driver's License Deadlines

New residents usually have a limited time to register their car and get a new driver's license. Each state sets its own deadline, commonly somewhere between a few weeks and a few months after you move. You'll typically need proof of insurance that meets the new state's requirements to register, which is another reason to sort out insurance first.

## Will Your Rate Go Up or Down?

It can go either way. Rates depend heavily on where you live, including your new state's rules and your new ZIP code's claims history, so a move across town can change your rate, and a move across the country can change it a lot. Our guide to [how your ZIP code affects your rate](/blogs/how-zip-code-affects-your-insurance-rate) explains why. A move is also a good moment to compare insurers, since the cheapest company in your old state may not be the cheapest in the new one.

## Temporary Moves: Students and Military

If your move is temporary, the rules can differ. College students who keep their permanent address at home, and military members stationed in another state, can sometimes keep a policy based in their home state. Ask your insurer before assuming, and tell them where the car will actually be kept.

## Your Moving Checklist

1. Call your insurer with your new address and moving date.
2. Check your new state's minimum coverage requirements.
3. Update your policy, or buy a new one that starts before the old one ends.
4. Register your car and get a new driver's license by your new state's deadline.
5. Compare quotes. Your move may have changed which insurer is cheapest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I keep my car insurance if I move to another state?

Sometimes. If your insurer operates in the new state, it can usually update your policy for that state. If it doesn't, you'll need a new insurer. Either way, your policy must reflect where the car is now kept.

### Do I need new insurance before I register my car in a new state?

Usually yes. Most states require proof of insurance that meets their own minimums to register a vehicle.

### Am I covered while driving to my new state?

Generally yes. U.S. auto policies cover you while driving in other states, so your current policy covers the trip. Update it once you've moved.

## Key takeaways

- Your car insurance is tied to where your car is kept, so a move to another state means updating or replacing your policy.
- Tell your insurer before you move, and keep your old policy active until the new one starts.
- Your new state may require different or higher coverage, and you'll need proof of insurance to register your car there.
- Registration and license deadlines vary by state, commonly a few weeks to a few months.
- A move can raise or lower your rate, so compare insurers once you know your new address.
